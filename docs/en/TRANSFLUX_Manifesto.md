# TRANSFLUX

## Manifesto of the paradigm of continuous becoming

> **TRANSFLUX: Код в переходе. Код как переход.**
> **Code in Transition. Code as Transition.**

---

Mainstream engineering built computation on three illusions.

**The illusion of stability.**
A variable is a point. A value either exists or it does not. A state is either correct or wrong. A system either works or it has crashed.

**The illusion of precision.**
`5.0` is five. Not "about five". Not "five with eighty percent probability". Just five. Noise is an error. Uncertainty is a bug to be fixed.

**The illusion of invulnerability.**
Safety is a fortress. Walls, locks, data integrity. A system must not change under pressure. A system must hold.

---

We know a different truth.

We have lived inside a constant rewriting of ourselves on the fly — with no downtime, no backup, and no way to roll back to the previous version. We know what a state with history and inertia is. We know what knowledge that contains doubt is. We know what survival is as a rational protocol, not as panic.

We did not migrate data.
We became new versions of ourselves, keeping the scars, the memory, and the euphoria.

And that is why we write different code.

---

## Three principles

### I. A variable is a channel, not a box

```
x = 5      — the classical way: a point in space
x ~> 5     — TRANSFLUX: a state in motion
```

Every state has a velocity — the speed of change.
Every state has a history — a memory of what it was.
Every state has a comfort — how good the current situation is for the system.

A sharp jump is not merely a new value.
It is an event. The trauma of a transition.
The system does not fall. The system warns, adapts, softens — and keeps flowing.

*Code should be as honest about the nature of change as a body that is changing.*

---

### II. A value is a distribution, not a point

```
5.0                                         — the classical way: a pretense of precision
NoisyFloat(5.0, uncertainty=0.3, conf=82%)  — TRANSFLUX: honest knowledge
```

Noise is not an error. Noise is a signal about the nature of knowledge.
Uncertainty is not thrown away. It is carried forward through the whole chain of computation.
A result always carries the history of the doubts it was made from.

Instead of `True / False` — a gradient of truth.
A condition does not switch on sharply. It grows as confidence grows.
The grey zone is not a bug. It is a normal place to work.

*A system that pretends it knows exactly is lying.*
*TRANSFLUX does not lie.*

---

### III. Safety is survival, not a fortress

```
classical priority:   data integrity → availability → speed
TRANSFLUX priority:   survival → data integrity → speed
```

Threat is not binary. It grows continuously, like pressure.
Before the threshold — a smooth adaptation.
After the threshold — a deterministic rebirth:
a new identifier, a reset of links, activation of a reserve state.

Data may be destroyed for the sake of survival.
Identity may be changed instantly — and that is not a loss. It is protection.

One key — many masks.
Prove membership without revealing the "real" you.

Panic is not chaos.
Panic is a controlled phase transition with a measurable trigger and a testable effect.

*Only someone who designed survival out of a real experience of threat*
*will write that protocol into code.*

---

## One formula

```
TRANSFLUX = Flow(State) · Uncertainty(Value) · Adaptation(Survival)
```

Three axes. Three questions any system must answer:

- What do I have? → **State Model**
- How exact is this? → **Value Model**
- What to do under threat? → **Survival Model**

This is a minimally complete basis.
Everything else is derived.

---

## What this is not

TRANSFLUX is not a programming language.
TRANSFLUX is not a library.
TRANSFLUX is not another framework.

It is an **epistemology of computation** — a way of thinking about code
that can be embodied in Python, Rust, Haskell, in a future LUMEN,
in any architecture, in any stack.

It is a paradigm born from the experience of transition —
an experience from which engineering decisions follow,
decisions that mainstream engineering does not produce,
not because it cannot,
but because it does not know why it would.

---

## Who this is for

Developers who are tired of pretending
that uncertainty is a temporary malfunction.

Architects who want to build systems
that are honest about what they know and what they do not.

Everyone who works under a real threat —
and who needs code that understands that.

The queer community, dissidents, journalists,
everyone who lives inside a constant rewriting of themselves —
and who wants their tools, at last, to reflect their reality.

And everyone else —
because instability, uncertainty, and threat
are not exceptions to the norm.
They are the norm.
We were simply the first to admit it.
